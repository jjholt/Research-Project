\section{Motivation}

\paragraph{Traditional stump prosthesis}
Following an amputation, there is often a functional need for a prosthesis.
Traditionally, these contain a socket wherein the residual limb (stump) is placed.
This leads to loss of bone density, and -- particularly for leg prostheses -- friction against the socket, uneven loads on the soft tissue, and other abuses cause severe skin problems \citep{levy_skin_1980}.

\paragraph{Intro to ITAP}
Intraosseous transcutaneous amputation prostheses (ITAP) \cite{kang_osseocutaneous_2010} (See Figure \ref{fig:ITAP}) provide amputees an alternative attachment of artificial limbs, bypassing the role of the residual limb's soft tissue.
The purpose of ITAP is to shift the load from the soft tissue to the skeleton, while achieving cutaneous integration.


\paragraph{Future of ITAP}
An amputation is not just the loss of a limb, but also the loss of tactile feedback and other information one gathers with that limb.
Generally, prosthetics users rely on visual feedback and secondary cues, such as sound or changes in pressure on the residual limb \cite{schofield_applications_2014}.
Sensory feedback, particularly vibrotactile, is associated with better object recognition, manipulation and grip force control \cite{schiefer_artificial_2018}\cite{rosenbaum-chou_development_2016}.
While sensory feedback must be achieved through electrical stimulation for stump prosthetics, we hypothesise that ITAP can be the vehicle for transmission of haptic feedback.
However, little is understood about the consequence of vibrations on either the skin-implant or the bone-implant interfaces.

Following amputation, and depending on the bone quality of the residuum, ITAP may be press-fitted or cemented into the remaining bone in the amputated limb.
It is unclear what effect this has on vibrations propagating through ITAP that are transmitted to the bone and surrounding soft tissue.
Therefore, this project aims to \textbf{investigate the effect that press-fitting and cementing have on the forces experienced by cells during vibrotactile feedback}.
These results will feed into a larger research project to ensure the safety and reliability of haptic feedback and its use in the development of cognitive control over artificial limbs.


\section{Procedure}
This project will investigate vibrotactile force propagation (exemplified in Figure \ref{fig:model-data}) along ITAP devices.
The initial computational modelling phase aims to determine the effect on vibrations of each of the following: the presence of the flange, different stem diameters, and the presence of bone cement.
It begins with a simple metal rod and builds up to an ITAP such that it is possible to parametise both its characteristics and the interface with bone.
Finally, it will ascertain whether there is a measurable difference between the forces at the ITAP-bone interface for cemented and press-fitted implants.

The subsequent protoyping phase aims to establish a threshold of perception for soft tissue and to validate the computational model.
This phase will progress similarly, beginning with the metal rod design and finishing with an ITAP of one particular dimension; emulating the ITAP-bone and ITAP-soft tissue interfaces.
