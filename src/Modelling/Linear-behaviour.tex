The experimental setup was essentially a fulcrum, therefore there were two ways of directly and linearly controlling the amplitude of vibrations: By changing the amplitude of the input, and by changing the position where the load is applied.

\subsection{Amplitude of input}
By increasing the amplitude of the input linearly, we saw the maximum displacements across all three sensors linearly increase with the same slope of approximately $ 10^{-4} $.
This is shown in Figure \ref{fig:max_displacement-input_amplitude}.

\subsection{Position of load}
We measured the position of the load starting at the base of the spigot and moved towards the fulcrum, which we called ``offset'', i.e. offset from the base of the spigot (See figure \ref{fig:offset-from-base} (b)).
On a physical model this referred to the position of the actuator and helped inform the size of the actuators we might use.

We observed a linear decline in the maximum displacement with an increase in offset.
The rate of change for each sensor was different, suggesting we may move the vibration motor for finer control over the relative amplitude of each part of the device.
This is shown in figure \ref{fig:offset-from-base} (a).
