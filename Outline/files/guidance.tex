% Intraosseous transcutaneous amputation prostheses (ITAP) provide an alternative means of attaching artificial limbs for amputees, bypassing the role of the stump soft tissues in the attachment. This conveys several significant advantages including the possibility of utilizing ITAP for developing cognitive control over artificial limbs for upper limb amputees. Little is known or understood about how haptics could be used in this application.

% Passing vibrations through ITAP could be used for artificial limb control mechanisms, however these vibrations could be detrimental to the skin-implant and bone-implant interfaces resulting in infection, possible aseptic loosening and ultimately, implant failure. Following amputation and depending on the bone quality of the residuum, ITAP can be press-fit or cemented into the remaining bone in the amputated limb – ultimately having an effect on the way vibrations are passed through ITAP and transmitted to the bone and soft tissues.

% This project will investigate the effect of press-fitting or cementing on the size and nature of the forces the cells at the ITAP-bone/soft tissue interfaces can encounter. The results of this study will feed into a larger research project to assess the effect of these forces on the biological implant interfaces between ITAP and their recipients to ensure that haptics are a safe and reliable means of controlling artificial limbs for amputees.
